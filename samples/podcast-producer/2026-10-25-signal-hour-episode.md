```
Pronunciation notes: Higgsfield (HIG-z-feeld). Legora (leh-GOR-ah). Levie (luh-VEE). Agrawal (AG-ruh-wahl). Naveen Rao (nah-VEEN rah-OH).

[music in]
HOST A: [upbeat] An AI video company says it crossed one billion dollars in annualized revenue eighteen months after launch. [pause] That is where we start this week.
[music out]

HOST A: [warm] Welcome to Signal Hour. It is October twenty-fifth, twenty twenty-six. This week we hear what AI startups say in their own words, and what the big business and tech podcasts are saying about the same moment. Two funding announcements from startups themselves, then four podcast conversations about how companies get built and where the money goes. A reminder before we start: when a company writes about itself, we say so, and when a guest predicts something, that is a prediction and not a measurement.
[long pause]

HOST A: First, Higgsfield. The AI video company announced an extension of its Series B, with new money from TCV and NVIDIA. On its own blog, Higgsfield says it crossed one billion dollars in annualized revenue run rate in September, eighteen months after launch.
HOST B: And the company says it has more than forty million users across two hundred thirty-eight countries and territories, and that enterprise growth is more than one hundred percent month over month. [measured] Those numbers come from Higgsfield itself, so read them as what the company reports.
HOST A: NVIDIA shows up twice in this story. Higgsfield says it is expanding to NVIDIA Vera Rubin to meet growing demand. It also says it uses NVIDIA Dynamo to deploy and scale custom inference workloads on its own GPU infrastructure.
HOST B: The obvious question is how long a run rate like that holds. Nothing in what we have answers it.
[Source: Higgsfield Blog, higgsfield.ai/blog/higgsfield-series-b-extension-tcv-nvidia]
[long pause]

HOST A: Next, Legora, the legal AI company. On its blog, Legora says it raised five hundred fifty million dollars in a Series E, at an eight point eight billion dollar post-money valuation.
HOST B: The strategy is the interesting part. Legora says it builds on the best frontier models and does not train its own general legal model. It says it was the first large legal AI company to offer consumption-based pricing. And it says it now employs more than one hundred fifty legal engineers who work inside customer teams.
HOST A: A company valued near nine billion dollars, and it chooses not to build its own model.
HOST B: That is our read, not their claim, but yes. It is a bet on the application, not the model. Compare that with Horowitz in a minute, who talks about proprietary data.
[Source: Legora Blog, legora.com/blog/scaling-fast-built-to-last]
[long pause]

HOST A: Now the podcasts. On the a16z show, Box CEO Aaron Levie says AI agents let a small team do the work of a big one. He says a team of three to ten people can have the output of a team of fifty to two hundred.
HOST B: He says the founder's job changes too. Founders become, quote, more managers of work, with the work done by agents. And he predicts there will be hundreds of new companies worth one billion to ten billion dollars or more, built on automating specific job functions with agents.
HOST A: He also says most AI progress will now go into enterprise use cases. And he says AI does not fundamentally transform the distribution model of tech.
HOST B: Which lines up with Higgsfield's enterprise growth number, and with Legora's consumption pricing. Again, those links are ours, not his.
[Source: The a16z Show, a16z.simplecast.com/episodes/the-new-startup-playbook-box-ceo-aaron-levie-I0FN5_nv]
[long pause]

HOST A: Same show, another day. Ben Horowitz says AI is, quote, a probabilistic computer, where earlier computing eras used deterministic computers.
HOST B: He argues that AI applications can reach massive user numbers instantly, because they do not rely on network-effect adoption curves. So where does the lasting advantage come from?
HOST A: His answer: proprietary data and customer possession, rather than just model architecture. He also says you do not take out a competitor with an equally good product. You need something dramatically better.
HOST B: [laughs] And for founders, he says you are not a great entrepreneur unless you feel you have no choice but to build the company.
[Source: The a16z Show, a16z.simplecast.com/episodes/what-it-takes-to-win-as-a-founder-ben-horowitz-AjG_7MSY]
[long pause]

HOST A: Two more voices on where this goes. On the 20VC podcast, Naveen Rao predicts the inference market will reach ten trillion dollars within ten years. He says robotics will be one hundred times larger than the market for coding agents.
HOST B: [measured] He also says energy supply is the primary bottleneck for AI scaling, and that pure software investing is becoming significantly less viable than hardware-centric AI investing. Those are one guest's predictions, not data.
HOST A: On the Sourcery podcast, former Twitter CEO Parag Agrawal predicts agents will eventually use the web five thousand to ten thousand times more than humans do. He says for every ten GPUs spent on model inference, the industry will spend one to two on web search.
HOST B: And he says today's web business models, built on human attention, are not durable in a web dominated by agents.
[Source: 20VC Podcast, Naveen Rao episode; Sourcery, Parag Agrawal episode. Links in show notes]
[long pause]

HOST A: And a caution. On the All-In podcast, a speaker says one percent of companies represent ninety percent of all AI spending. And of that ninety percent, the speaker says, eighty percent comes from unprofitable, venture-backed companies.
HOST B: [measured] That is a claim made on a podcast, not an audited figure. The same episode says enterprises are gravitating toward open-source and open-weight models, to get explainability, auditability and verifiability.
[Source: Official All-In Podcast, Nikesh Arora episode. Link in show notes]
[long pause]

HOST A: Quick wrap. Higgsfield says it passed one billion dollars in run rate. Legora raised five hundred fifty million and builds on other companies' models.
HOST B: Levie says small teams of founders will manage agents, and Horowitz says lasting advantage comes from proprietary data. Rao and Agrawal predict huge growth in inference and in agent traffic. And All-In says AI spending is very concentrated.
HOST A: [warm] That is Signal Hour. Sources are in the show notes. See you next week.
[music in] [music out]
```
